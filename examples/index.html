The MoMA Rules

Ten laws for a screen that does not show its builder was an amateur. Every law has a check that fails the build, because the previous three versions of these rules were prose, and prose does not execute.

“MoMA rules dictate that everything has to be aligned and there should be no loose edges to show that the builder is an amateur.” Dr Non · 2026-08-20
I

One origin

Every left edge on a page descends from one value. The corollary costs the most: a hairline may not occupy layout space. A 1px border pushes every child inward. The same line drawn with box-shadow: inset does not.

II

The scale

Fifteen spacing values. There is no sixteenth. Dense where decisions are frequent, sparse where they are rare — a linear scale wastes resolution at the top.

In red: values found in production that are on no scale — 1 · 3 · 5 · 7 · 9 · 35 · 37 · 38 · 95 · 97 · 118 · 222. Measured across src/: 86 distinct spacing values, 40.1% off any 4px grid.

IV

The grid fills

A grid of C columns holds N items where N mod C is zero. Twelve is the friendliest count on a page: it divides by 2, 3, 4 and 6. Curate the universe to fit the grid, never pad the grid to fit the universe.

Twelve cells, four columns, three full rows. Resize the window: at every width this grid resolves to 1, 2, 3 or 4 columns — all divisors of twelve — so it never leaves an orphan.

V

One rhythm per page

VII

No near miss

Two edges are the same, or they are clearly different. The band between is what the eye catches and calls amateur — and it is invisible in code review, which is why prose-only design passes never caught it.
